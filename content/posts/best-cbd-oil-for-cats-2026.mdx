## Is CBD Safe for Cats? What the Research Says

This is the right question to ask first. The honest answer: we have less clinical data for cats than for dogs, but what exists is encouraging.

**What we know:**
- A 2021 pharmacokinetic study published in *JVIM* found CBD was well-tolerated in cats at typical doses, with no serious adverse events
- Cats have a lower metabolic capacity for some compounds due to reduced glucuronidation in the liver — this means dosing conservatively and using cat-specific products is important
- The most commonly reported side effects are sedation and GI upset — both dose-dependent and reversible

**What we don't know:**
- Long-term safety data in cats is limited
- Specific therapeutic dose-response data for feline anxiety or pain is still emerging

**Bottom line:** CBD appears safe for cats at appropriate doses from reputable sources, but this is a "talk to your vet first" area, especially if your cat is on other medications (CBD can affect drug metabolism).

## What to Look for in Cat CBD Products

Before reviewing specific brands, here's the evaluation framework:

**Non-negotiables:**
- Certificate of Analysis (COA) from an independent, accredited lab — verifies CBD content and confirms absence of heavy metals, pesticides, THC above 0.3%
- THC-free or broad-spectrum (not full-spectrum with significant THC) — cats are more sensitive to THC toxicity than dogs
- Cat-appropriate dosing guidance — not just "scale down the dog dose"
- No xylitol, essential oils (toxic to cats), or artificial sweeteners in the formula

**Nice to have:**
- Published research or vet involvement in formulation
- Small, graduated dosing options (tinctures allow more precision than capsules)
- Palatability tested with cats

## Before You Buy: Talk to Your Vet

CBD can inhibit certain liver enzymes (cytochrome P450), which affects how the body processes other medications. If your cat takes:

- Phenobarbital (seizure control)
- Cyclosporine (immune suppression)
- Any other medications metabolized by the liver

...discuss CBD with your vet before starting. This isn't a theoretical concern — the interaction is well-documented in cats and dogs.
