## What Actually Matters in Cat Litter

Before the comparison, here's what the research and cat owner reviews consistently show matters most:

- **Odor control**: The #1 reason cats reject their box is inadequate odor management — their noses are 14× more sensitive than ours
- **Dust levels**: Fine clay dust contains crystalline silica, a respiratory irritant; low-dust formulas matter for cats and owners with asthma
- **Clumping vs. non-clumping**: Clumping makes scooping efficient and reduces bacterial growth; non-clumping requires full box changes more often
- **Cost per month**: Litter cost varies wildly depending on waste ratio and how often the full box needs replacing
- **Tracking**: How much litter your cat drags out of the box affects your cleaning routine significantly

## #3 Breeze Litter System — Best for Zero Tracking and Easy Maintenance

The Breeze system by Tidy Cats takes a completely different approach: large zeolite pellets sit in an elevated tray above an absorbent pad. Urine falls through the pellets to the pad below; solids stay on top for easy scooping. It's not traditional litter — it's a system.

### How the Breeze System Works

The pellets don't absorb urine — they just separate it downward to the pad. The pad absorbs and contains urine odor for about a week (for one cat). The pellets themselves control ammonia and solid waste odor and last about a month before replacement.

Weekly cost: one pad per week (~$1.25–$1.50). Monthly cost: approximately one 3.5-lb bag of pellets ($8–$12).

### Who the Breeze System Is For

**Near-zero tracking.** The large zeolite pellets don't stick to paws the way clay dust does. If tracking litter through your home is your top complaint, Breeze eliminates it almost entirely.

**Low dust.** The pellets produce essentially no dust — important for cats with asthma or owners with respiratory conditions.

**Low daily maintenance.** No daily litter clump breakdown. Scoop solids daily, swap the pad weekly. The box doesn't require full emptying and scrubbing as often as clay.

### Limitations

The Breeze system requires buying into the proprietary ecosystem — Breeze pads and Breeze pellets only. Some cats reject the pellets, particularly those accustomed to soft clay. If your cat refuses the Breeze tray, you've invested in a system you can't use.

- Good for small spaces (no tracking mess)

- Some cats refuse the pellets

## FAQ

**Q: Is World's Best Cat Litter actually safe to flush?**
A: The manufacturer says yes, and the corn-based formula is biodegradable. However, some municipalities prohibit flushing any cat waste due to *Toxoplasma gondii* contamination concerns in waterways. Check your local guidelines before flushing.

**Q: How often should I completely change the litter box?**
A: With clumping litter (World's Best or Fresh Step): full box change every 2–4 weeks with daily scooping. With Breeze: pellet change monthly, pad change weekly.

**Q: My cat refuses the new litter. How do I transition them?**
A: Introduce new litter gradually by mixing 25% new with 75% old for one week, then 50/50 the next week, then 75/25. Never switch 100% overnight. For Breeze, some cats need the pellets mixed with a familiar fine clay in a transitional tray.

**Q: Is clay litter harmful to cats?**
A: Traditional clay litters, especially sodium bentonite clumping clay, can cause intestinal obstruction in kittens who ingest large amounts. For kittens under 4 months, non-clumping or corn-based litters are safer.
