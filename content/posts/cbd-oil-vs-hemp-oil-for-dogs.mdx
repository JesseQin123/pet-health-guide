# CBD Oil vs Hemp Oil for Dogs: What's the Difference?

Walk into any pet store or browse Amazon, and you'll find shelves stocked with "hemp oil for dogs" and "CBD oil for dogs" — often side by side, sometimes at wildly different prices. Are they the same thing? Which one does your dog actually need?

This confusion is one of the most common in the pet supplement world, and it costs pet owners real money when they buy the wrong product. This guide clarifies the difference, explains what each one does, and tells you exactly which situations call for which product.

**Hemp seed oil** and **CBD oil** are completely different products made from different parts of the hemp plant with different active compounds and different effects.

## What Is Hemp Seed Oil?

Hemp seed oil is pressed cold from the seeds of the hemp plant — the same mechanical process used to make olive oil or coconut oil. Hemp seeds contain virtually no cannabinoids (no CBD, no THC). What they do contain is an exceptional **omega-3 to omega-6 fatty acid profile**.

### What Hemp Seed Oil Does for Dogs

- **Skin and coat health**: The omega-6 gamma-linolenic acid (GLA) in hemp seed oil is particularly beneficial for dry, itchy skin and a dull coat. Many dogs with skin allergies see improvement with consistent hemp seed oil supplementation.
- **General omega-3 supplementation**: Hemp seeds have a 3:1 omega-6 to omega-3 ratio, which is favorable. However, plant-based omega-3 (ALA) must be converted to EPA and DHA by the body — dogs do this conversion inefficiently. Fish oil remains superior for dogs needing meaningful EPA/DHA.
- **Cardiovascular support**: The essential fatty acid profile supports healthy blood vessels and heart function.
- **Anti-inflammatory**: ALA has mild anti-inflammatory effects — much less potent than EPA/DHA from fish oil, but a useful addition to a supplement stack.

### What Hemp Seed Oil Does NOT Do

Hemp seed oil has **zero effect on the endocannabinoid system**. It will not:
- Reduce anxiety
- Relieve chronic pain
- Reduce seizure frequency
- Provide the therapeutic effects associated with CBD research

If you're giving your dog "hemp oil" thinking it will help with anxiety or arthritis — but it's actually hemp seed oil — you have purchased the wrong product.

## What Is CBD Oil?

CBD oil is extracted from the **flowers, leaves, and stalks** of the hemp plant, not the seeds. These parts of the plant contain high concentrations of cannabidiol (CBD) and other beneficial cannabinoids and terpenes.

The extract is typically mixed with a carrier oil — usually MCT coconut oil or hemp seed oil — for stability and absorption. This is why some CBD products look like hemp seed oil and are even labeled "hemp extract with hemp seed oil." The key active ingredient is the CBD, not the carrier.

### How CBD Works in Dogs

CBD interacts with the **endocannabinoid system (ECS)** — a regulatory network present in all mammals that modulates:

- **Mood and anxiety**: CB1 receptors in the brain
- **Pain and inflammation**: CB1 and CB2 receptors throughout the body
- **Immune function**: CB2 receptors on immune cells
- **Sleep and appetite**: Various ECS components

This is why CBD has potential applications that hemp seed oil simply cannot touch. Research in dogs has specifically examined CBD for:
- **Osteoarthritis**: A Cornell University study found 2 mg/kg twice daily significantly reduced pain scores
- **Epilepsy**: A Colorado State University trial showed reduced seizure frequency
- **Anxiety**: Multiple preclinical studies support anxiolytic effects

## A Common Label Trick: Watch Out for This

Some brands label products as "hemp oil" or "hemp extract" without specifying whether it's CBD-containing hemp extract or plain hemp seed oil. This is often intentional — they charge CBD prices for hemp seed oil.

**How to tell the difference:**
1. **Check the label for "cannabidiol" or "CBD"** — if you don't see these words, you likely have hemp seed oil
2. **Look for mg dosing** — CBD products state total CBD in mg (e.g., "300 mg CBD per bottle"). Hemp seed oil lists serving size in ml.
3. **Check the price** — genuine full-spectrum CBD at 300 mg per bottle costs $30–$60+. If it's $10, it's probably hemp seed oil.
4. **Request the COA** — a real CBD product has a cannabinoid panel showing CBD content. Hemp seed oil has no cannabinoids to report.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can hemp seed oil cause a dog to fail a drug test?**
No. Hemp seed oil contains no cannabinoids and cannot cause a positive drug test.

**Can CBD oil cause a dog to get high?**
No — provided it's hemp-derived with <0.3% THC. Quality CBD products for dogs are typically broad-spectrum (zero THC) or full-spectrum (trace THC that is not psychoactive at normal doses). THC is toxic to dogs; always verify your product's THC content.

**Is hemp seed oil safe for dogs?**
Yes, hemp seed oil is safe for dogs. It's essentially a food-grade oil. Start with a small amount to introduce any new fat source — too much too quickly can cause loose stools.

**How much CBD oil should I give my dog?**
A starting dose of 0.25 mg CBD per pound of body weight, twice daily. Read our detailed guide: [How Long Does CBD Take to Work in Dogs?](/blog/how-long-does-cbd-take-to-work-in-dogs)

**My dog was getting hemp seed oil — should I switch to CBD?**
If you were giving hemp seed oil for anxiety or pain and it "wasn't working," that's because hemp seed oil can't address those concerns. Yes, switch to CBD oil from a quality brand.
