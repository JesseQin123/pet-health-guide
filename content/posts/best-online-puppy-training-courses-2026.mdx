# Best Online Puppy Training Courses 2026 — Baxter & Bella vs. K9 Training Institute

A new puppy is a 10-to-15-year commitment that starts with a 12-week window you can't afford to waste. The socialization period (roughly 3–14 weeks) shapes your dog's worldview permanently — and the foundations laid in the first 6 months determine whether you'll spend the next decade enjoying your dog or managing a problem.

In-person puppy classes run $200–$400 for a 6-week course. A private trainer costs $75–$150 per session. Online courses have become the serious alternative — not because they're cheaper (the best ones aren't cheap), but because they're available when you need them, designed to be worked through at your pace, and increasingly backed by the same behavioral science that guides certified trainers.

Two programs stand out in 2026: **Baxter & Bella** and the **K9 Training Institute**. We completed both with real puppies and have an honest take on which one delivers.

## FAQ

### At what age should I start puppy training?

Training begins the day your puppy comes home — typically 8 weeks. The first week is about settling in; structured training starts week 2. The socialization window closes around 14–16 weeks, making that the highest-priority period for exposure work.

### Are online puppy training courses as effective as in-person classes?

For motivated owners, yes — and sometimes better. Online courses allow you to train at the optimal moment (when your puppy is calm and focused), revisit lessons, and work at your puppy's pace rather than a class schedule. The limiting factor is owner consistency, not course format.

### How long per day should I train my puppy?

2–5 minutes, 3–5 times per day. Puppy attention spans are short. Multiple short sessions outperform one long session in both retention and engagement. End every session before your puppy checks out — always finish on success.

### Is positive reinforcement really better than correction-based training?

The scientific consensus is yes, for behavioral outcomes and welfare. Positive reinforcement builds trust, maintains engagement, and produces dogs that want to work with you. Correction-based approaches can suppress behaviors without teaching alternatives, and increase the risk of fear-based aggression. The AVSAB, APDT, and IAABC all support positive-reinforcement-based training as the standard of care.

### What treats should I use for puppy training?

High-value, small, and quick to eat. Cooked chicken breast cut into pea-sized pieces is the gold standard. Avoid training with kibble for a hungry puppy — use their appetite for real reinforcers. As behaviors become reliable, you can fade treat frequency (but never fully eliminate them).

### How do I know if a training course is using outdated methods?

Warning signs: any mention of "alpha," "dominant," "pack leader," "show them who's boss," or physical corrections. These concepts are rooted in debunked 1970s wolf-pack research and are not supported by modern behavioral science. All reputable programs in 2026 use positive reinforcement as their primary method.
