## What Makes a Dog Probiotic Actually Work?

Before buying, look for:

1. **CFU count**: At least 1 billion CFU per dose. Under 500 million CFU is typically too low for clinical benefit.
2. **Multiple strains**: Lactobacillus acidophilus, L. rhamnosus, Bifidobacterium animalis, and Enterococcus faecium are the most researched for dogs.
3. **Survivability**: Probiotics must survive stomach acid to reach the gut. Look for products with "enteric coating" or that are proven shelf-stable.
4. **Third-party testing**: NASC seal or NSF certification means someone verified the CFU count on the label is accurate.

## When to Use a Dog Probiotic

Dog probiotics are most beneficial for:

- **After antibiotics**: Antibiotics kill both bad and good bacteria. Start a probiotic during the antibiotic course and continue for 2-4 weeks after.
- **Chronic loose stools or gas**: A disrupted gut microbiome is often the cause.
- **Yeast infections** (ears, paws, skin): Certain probiotic strains suppress yeast overgrowth.
- **Seasonal allergies**: Research shows gut microbiome diversity correlates with allergy severity in dogs.
- **Food transitions**: Changing your dog's food too quickly disrupts gut flora — a probiotic smooths the transition.
- **Stress**: Travel, boarding, new home — stress disrupts the gut-brain axis in dogs just like humans.

---

## Are Dog Probiotics Safe?

Yes, for healthy dogs, probiotics are generally very safe. The most common side effects in the first few days are gas or slightly looser stools as the gut adjusts.

Dogs with compromised immune systems (on chemotherapy, severe illness) should use probiotics only under veterinary guidance.
