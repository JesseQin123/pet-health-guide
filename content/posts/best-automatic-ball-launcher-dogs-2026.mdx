## Automatic Ball Launcher Buying Guide

### Teaching Your Dog to Use the Launcher

The biggest hurdle isn't the machine — it's getting your dog to understand the game. Most dogs master self-fetch in 1–3 sessions with these steps:

1. **Introduce the machine off.** Let your dog sniff and investigate it with treats placed around and inside it.
2. **Demonstrate the launch manually.** Drop a ball in while your dog watches — the machine does the rest. Praise and treat when your dog retrieves the ball.
3. **Guide the return.** Point to the machine when your dog returns with the ball. Treat heavily when they drop it in.
4. **Fade guidance.** Gradually reduce your pointing and treating as the dog learns the loop.

Most dogs catch on within 10–20 repetitions. High-drive fetch dogs may understand in 3–5.

### Indoor vs Outdoor Launchers

Indoor play requires **short range** (10–20 feet) and a **compact design**. Outdoor play benefits from **longer range** (30–40 feet) and weather resistance. Some launchers like the iFetch Too handle both well; the GoDogGo is primarily outdoor; the Paws & Pals is primarily indoor.

### Ball Size Compatibility

- **Standard tennis balls (2.5"):** Work in iFetch Too, GoDogGo, Hyper Pet, Paws & Pals — and with your existing ball supply
- **Mini balls (1.5"):** Required for iFetch Original — proprietary iFetch brand works best
- **Never use balls that are too small for your dog's mouth** — choking hazard

### Power: Battery vs AC Adapter

Battery-only launchers (some budget models) run 2–4 hours before needing replacement batteries — fine for one play session but expensive and inconvenient over time. Look for AC adapter compatibility. The iFetch Too, iFetch Original, and Paws & Pals all support AC power.

## FAQ: Automatic Ball Launchers

**Can any dog use an automatic ball launcher?**
Dogs with a natural fetch drive adapt quickest. Dogs who don't naturally retrieve usually need more training before the self-fetch loop clicks. Some dogs never fully engage — the Hyper Pet's lower price point makes it the right test before investing $200.

**Is it safe to let my dog play unsupervised with an automatic launcher?**
Most manufacturers recommend supervised use, especially initially. Once you're confident your dog plays safely (not chewing the machine, not overexerting), brief unsupervised sessions are generally fine. Use the rest timer feature if available to prevent overexertion.

**Do automatic ball launchers work for all dog sizes?**
Standard models work for dogs 20–100+ lbs. Small dogs (under 15 lbs) should use the iFetch Original with its mini balls. Very large dogs (over 100 lbs) may handle standard launchers more roughly — the iFetch Too is the most durable at this size.

**How loud are automatic ball launchers?**
Typical sound level is 60–75 dB during launch — comparable to a normal conversation. They're suitable for apartment use (neighbors won't hear through walls), though dogs may be startled by the sound on first exposure.

**How do I clean the launcher?**
Wipe the ball return funnel with a damp cloth weekly. Tennis balls accumulate dog saliva and dirt that can jam the mechanism — keep a fresh supply of balls in rotation and retire visibly deteriorated balls.
