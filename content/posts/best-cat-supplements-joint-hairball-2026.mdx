# Best Cat Supplements for Joint Health and Hairballs 2026

Cats are masters at hiding discomfort. By the time you notice your 10-year-old tabby moving more slowly or grooming less, joint inflammation may have been building for months. And hairballs — while often dismissed as "just a cat thing" — can signal digestive tract issues worth addressing proactively.

The right supplements won't replace veterinary care, but they can meaningfully slow joint degeneration, ease hairball passage, and improve daily quality of life for cats at any age. We reviewed the top options on the market for 2026, with an eye toward clinical evidence, ingredient quality, and palatability (because the best supplement is the one your cat will actually eat).

## Joint Supplements for Cats: What Actually Works

Before diving into product picks, a quick primer on ingredients to look for — and ones to skip.

**Evidence-backed ingredients:**
- **Glucosamine HCl** — supports cartilage repair and joint fluid production
- **Chondroitin sulfate** — helps cartilage retain water and resist compression
- **Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA)** — reduces joint inflammation at the source
- **Green-lipped mussel** — contains a unique combination of fatty acids and glycosaminoglycans
- **UC-II collagen** — clinical trials show efficacy at lower doses than traditional glucosamine/chondroitin

**Skip these:**
- Supplements with high filler content or proprietary blends that obscure actual dosages
- Products from manufacturers with no NASC (National Animal Supplement Council) seal
- Anything making cure or disease-treatment claims (red flag for regulatory compliance)

## Best Hairball Supplements for Cats 2026

Hairballs form when cats ingest loose fur during grooming, and the fur accumulates in the stomach. Most cats will hack up occasional hairballs, but frequent hairballs (more than once or twice a month) or retching without producing a hairball can indicate a dietary fiber issue or GI motility problem worth addressing.

### How Hairball Supplements Work

Hairball supplements work through one of two mechanisms:

1. **Lubricant-based** (petroleum jelly or mineral oil) — coat ingested hair so it passes through the GI tract rather than accumulating
2. **Fiber-based** (psyllium, pumpkin, chicory root) — add bulk to move hair through the gut naturally

Fiber-based approaches are generally preferred by integrative vets as they address the root cause (gut motility) rather than just greasing the passage.

## Signs Your Cat May Need a Joint Supplement

- Hesitating before jumping onto furniture they previously used easily
- Stiff gait, especially after sleeping
- Reduced grooming (can't reach all areas comfortably)
- Less interest in play or interactive activities
- Behavioral changes: increased irritability or hiding

If you notice multiple signs, consult your vet before starting supplements — X-rays can confirm whether osteoarthritis is the cause and guide treatment intensity.
