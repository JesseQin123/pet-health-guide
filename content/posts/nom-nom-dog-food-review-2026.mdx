Nom Nom entered the fresh dog food market in 2015 with a simple pitch: cook nutritionally complete, human-grade dog food with real ingredients, portion it exactly for each dog's size and calorie needs, and ship it weekly. Purina acquired them in 2020, which raised some eyebrows — but also brought veterinary nutritionist oversight and food safety infrastructure to the brand.

We fed Nom Nom to 6 dogs for 8 weeks: a 7 lb Maltese, a 22 lb Beagle, a 38 lb Border Collie, a 52 lb Labrador, a 68 lb German Shepherd, and a 90 lb Bernese Mountain Dog. Here's an honest look at what we found.

## Ingredients and Nutrition

Nom Nom's four current recipes are:

**Beef Mash:** Ground beef, russet potatoes, eggs, carrots, peas, sunflower oil, fish oil, Nom Nom nutrient mix

**Chicken Cuisine:** Ground chicken, brown rice, carrots, eggs, spinach, sunflower oil, fish oil, Nom Nom nutrient mix

**Turkey Fare:** Ground turkey, brown rice, butternut squash, eggs, kale, sunflower oil, fish oil, Nom Nom nutrient mix

**Pork Potluck:** Ground pork, yellow squash, eggs, brown rice, spinach, sunflower oil, fish oil, Nom Nom nutrient mix

All recipes include Nom Nom's proprietary nutrient mix — a blend of vitamins and minerals that brings each recipe to AAFCO complete-and-balanced status. This is the same approach used by all fresh dog food companies, as fresh food alone doesn't contain adequate micronutrient levels.

**What's missing:** No artificial preservatives, artificial colors, or fillers. Grain-inclusive (not grain-free) — which aligns with current FDA guidance on potential links between grain-free diets and dilated cardiomyopathy.

## Our 8-Week Test Results

### Coat and Skin: Noticeable Improvement

By week 4, four of our six test dogs showed visibly shinier coats. The Border Collie and German Shepherd (both with historically dry coats) showed the most dramatic improvement. The omega fatty acids from fish oil in every recipe are the likely driver.

**Rating: 4.5/5**

### Energy and Digestion: Mostly Positive

Most dogs had normal, well-formed stools within 7–10 days of transitioning. Two dogs (Labrador and Bernese) had loose stools in days 3–5 — a normal transition response when switching from high-fiber kibble to fresh food. Both normalized by day 10.

Energy levels were consistent and high for all six dogs throughout the test.

**Rating: 4/5**

### Palatability: Near-Universal

All six dogs ate their Nom Nom meals without hesitation. The Maltese, who typically grazes and leaves kibble in the bowl, cleaned the Nom Nom pouch within 2 minutes every meal.

**Rating: 5/5**

### Convenience: Solid, with Minor Friction

The pre-portioned pouches eliminate guesswork. Tear, squeeze into bowl, done. Pouches are refrigerator-stable for 7 days or freezer-stable for 4 months. The packaging is a significant amount of plastic per week for larger dogs.

The biggest friction point: remembering to defrost frozen pouches overnight. If you forget and try to serve a frozen pouch, you'll be running it under warm water for 15 minutes.

**Rating: 3.5/5**

## Who Should Try Nom Nom

**Best candidates:**
- Dogs with dull coats, itchy skin, or seasonal allergies

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Nom Nom the same as Purina?**

Nom Nom was acquired by Purina in 2020 but continues to operate as a separate brand with its own recipes and nutritional team. Purina's manufacturing standards and food safety oversight now apply.

**How long do the pouches last in the fridge?**

7 days refrigerated, 4 months frozen. Nom Nom ships with enough ice packs to keep food cold during transit.

**Can I mix Nom Nom with kibble?**

Yes — Nom Nom explicitly supports 50% or 25% mixing. Their website calculates the right combination for your dog's calorie needs.

**What happens if my dog doesn't like it?**

Nom Nom offers a money-back guarantee on the first order. Contact their customer service within 30 days.

**Is Nom Nom good for puppies?**

Yes — all Nom Nom recipes are AAFCO complete and balanced "for all life stages," which includes puppies. Large-breed puppies should have calcium-phosphorus ratios monitored — confirm with your vet.
