## ⚠️ Critical Safety Warning Before You Buy

**Never use dog flea products on cats.** Specifically avoid any product containing:
- **Permethrin** — toxic to cats, even in trace amounts (grooming a treated dog can expose cats)
- **Pyrethrin at high concentrations** — found in some sprays and dips
- **Phenothrin** — another pyrethroid toxic to cats

If you have both dogs and cats, do not let your cat near a recently treated dog. Separate them for 24–72 hours after any dog flea treatment.

## How to Apply Cat Flea Topicals Safely

1. **Part the fur** at the base of the skull, between the shoulder blades (a spot your cat cannot reach to lick)
2. **Apply directly to skin**, not fur — the product absorbs through the skin
3. **Do not touch** the application site for 24 hours
4. **Keep away from treated dogs** for 24–72 hours if permethrin was used on the dog
5. **Wash your hands** immediately after application

## Treating Your Home

A cat infestation means 95% of the fleas (eggs, larvae, pupae) are in your environment, not on your cat. Along with monthly cat treatment:

- **Wash all bedding** in hot water weekly during active infestation
- **Vacuum daily** — dispose of the bag immediately outside
- **Use an IGR spray** (like Vet-Kem Siphotrol) on carpets and furniture
- **Treat all pets** in the household simultaneously

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use dog flea treatment on my cat?**
Never. Permethrin and other pyrethroids in dog products are toxic to cats and can be fatal even in small doses.

**How quickly does flea treatment work?**
Capstar kills adult fleas in 30 minutes. Topical treatments typically achieve 90%+ kill rate within 12–24 hours.

**My indoor cat has fleas — how?**
Fleas can enter on your clothing, shoes, or other pets. Even strictly indoor cats need prevention in endemic areas.

**Can I use flea shampoo on my cat?**
Only cat-specific flea shampoos with pyrethrin (not permethrin). Natural options like dish soap can remove adult fleas temporarily but provide no ongoing prevention.

**How long until the infestation is gone?**
With consistent monthly treatment and environmental control, most infestations clear in 2–3 months (accounting for the full flea life cycle).
