# Rover vs. Wag vs. PetSitter.com: Best Dog Boarding App 2026

Planning a vacation is stressful enough. Finding someone you trust to watch your dog shouldn't add to it. Dog boarding apps have transformed the industry — instead of expensive kennels or imposing on friends, you can book a vetted, reviewed dog sitter in your neighborhood in minutes.

But **Rover**, **Wag**, and **PetSitter.com** work very differently. The right choice depends on where you live, your dog's needs, and how much you want to pay. Here's everything you need to know before your next trip.

## Rover

<a href="[AFFILIATE_LINK_ROVER]" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">Rover</a> is the largest pet services marketplace in the world, with over 500,000 sitters across 50+ countries. That scale matters: in most US cities and suburbs, you'll find dozens of sitters within a few miles, giving you real choice on price, experience, and specialization.

**How Rover works:** You search by service type, dates, and location. Rover shows sitters with photos, reviews, rates, and a brief bio. You message a few sitters, pick one you like, and book through the platform. Payment is held until 24 hours after the service starts — a protection that neither Wag nor PetSitter.com matches.

**What Rover does well:**

- **Sitter depth:** Many Rover sitters are experienced dog professionals — trainers, vet techs, shelter volunteers — with years of reviews. Filtering by specialization (large dogs, puppies, medical needs) is easy.
- **Premium Protection:** Rover's insurance covers up to $25,000 in vet bills if your dog is injured while in a sitter's care. It's not a guarantee, but it's meaningful coverage.
- **Repeat bookings:** Once you find a sitter you trust, rebooking is frictionless. Rover keeps your sitter history and lets you message directly.

**Where Rover falls short:** Rover takes a 15–20% commission, which means sitters price accordingly. You'll generally pay more on Rover than comparable PetSitter.com listings. And in rural areas, sitter availability can be thin.

**Best for:** Most dog owners in suburban or urban areas who want a large pool of vetted sitters with strong platform protections.

## Wag

<a href="[AFFILIATE_LINK_WAG]" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">Wag</a> is Rover's closest competitor and is particularly strong for dog walking and drop-in visits. Where Rover excels at boarding, Wag's real differentiation is its on-demand model: you can book a walk often within an hour, making it useful for busy professionals who need same-day coverage.

**How Wag works:** Wag matches you with available sitters and walkers via GPS. For boarding, you choose a specific sitter. For walks, Wag sometimes assigns the nearest available walker — which creates inconsistency for dogs that need a familiar face.

**What Wag does well:**

- **Real-time GPS tracking:** Every walk comes with live GPS tracking and a report card with photos. This is Wag's clearest edge over Rover for walks — the transparency is excellent.
- **On-demand availability:** For same-day walks or drop-ins, Wag's on-demand model often wins. Rover's advance-booking model doesn't serve urgent requests as well.
- **$1M liability insurance:** Wag's insurance coverage is higher than Rover's on the liability side, though vet cost coverage caps are lower.

**Where Wag falls short:** Wag's 20–40% platform fee pushes its prices higher relative to the sitter's take-home — meaning you may pay more for a sitter who earns less, creating incentive misalignment. Reviews are mixed on sitter quality consistency, especially for the on-demand walk model. For boarding specifically, Rover's larger sitter pool and review depth give it the edge.

**Best for:** Dog owners who need on-demand or same-day walks and drop-ins, especially in major cities.

## PetSitter.com

<a href="[AFFILIATE_LINK_PETSITTER]" rel="nofollow sponsored" target="_blank">PetSitter.com</a> is the oldest of the three and operates more like a classifieds directory than a managed marketplace. Pet sitters pay an annual listing fee to appear in search results; there's no platform vetting beyond what sitters self-report.

**What PetSitter.com does well:**

- **Lower prices:** Without a 15–40% platform commission baked in, PetSitter.com sitters often charge less for equivalent services. If budget is a priority and you're willing to do more research upfront, the savings can be meaningful.
- **Direct relationships:** Because bookings often happen off-platform, you build a more direct relationship with the sitter — and can negotiate repeat-client rates.

**Where PetSitter.com falls short:**

- **No platform vetting:** PetSitter.com doesn't run background checks on sitters. You're responsible for verifying credentials, asking for references, and assessing safety yourself.
- **No insurance or dispute resolution:** If something goes wrong, PetSitter.com has no mechanism to help. This is a significant risk for boarding situations.
- **Weaker app experience:** The booking experience is clunkier than Rover or Wag. No GPS tracking, no standardized photo updates, no in-app messaging.

**Best for:** Owners in rural areas with limited Rover/Wag coverage, or experienced pet owners who prefer direct relationships and can do their own vetting.

## Which Dog Boarding App Should You Choose?

**Choose Rover if:** You want the largest sitter pool, the strongest platform protections, and a proven track record. Best for most people, most situations.

**Choose Wag if:** You need same-day or on-demand walks and drop-ins, and you live in a major city with high Wag coverage density.

**Choose PetSitter.com if:** Rover and Wag have limited coverage in your area, or you want to build a direct relationship with a local sitter and don't mind doing your own vetting.

## Tips for Finding a Great Dog Sitter

**Meet before you book.** Most platforms allow meet-and-greet visits before committing. Use them — watch how the sitter interacts with your dog. Body language tells you more than reviews.

**Read the reviews carefully.** Look for sitters who mention breed-specific experience if relevant. Reviews mentioning dogs like yours are the most predictive.

**Ask the right questions.** How many dogs do they board at once? Where will your dog sleep? What happens in a medical emergency? A good sitter answers confidently and specifically.

**Start with a trial night.** Before leaving your dog for a full week, book one overnight to see how your dog does and how the sitter communicates.
