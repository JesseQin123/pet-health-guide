## What Makes a Good Online Dog Training Course?

Before the reviews, here's the framework we used to evaluate each program:

- **Training philosophy**: Positive reinforcement vs. dominance/correction-based approaches (the former is strongly supported by behavioral science; the latter carries risks of increased anxiety and aggression)
- **Curriculum structure**: Is it logical, progressive, and easy to follow?
- **Video quality**: Can you actually see what the trainer is doing with the dog?
- **Community support**: Is there somewhere to ask questions when you're stuck?
- **Results**: Do real dogs improve on these methods?
- **Value**: Cost vs. comparable in-person training

## A Note on Training Methods

All courses we recommend use positive reinforcement-based methods (reward desired behavior, redirect or ignore undesired behavior). We do not recommend programs that rely primarily on corrections, prong collars, e-collars as training tools, or dominance theory.

The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior (AVSAB) position statement on training methods: "AVSAB recommends that only reward-based training methods are used for all animal training and behavior modification."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I train an old dog with online courses?**
Absolutely. Brain Training for Dogs specifically addresses the myth that old dogs can't learn — their University and Einstein modules include case studies with dogs over 10 years old.

**How much time does training take?**
Plan for 5–15 minutes per day of dedicated training. Consistency matters far more than duration.

**What if I'm not seeing results?**
Both Brain Training for Dogs (60-day guarantee) and Doggy Dan (varying refund policy) offer money-back options. Additionally, online training works best as a supplement to — not replacement for — real-world application.
