## Dog Harness Buying Guide

### Front-Clip vs Back-Clip vs Dual-Clip

**Back-clip harnesses** are most comfortable for well-trained, calm dogs. The leash attaches at the back, giving a natural gait and no pressure on the chest when standing still. Downside: they give you less control over pullers.

**Front-clip harnesses** redirect your dog toward you when they pull forward. This steering effect makes walking much easier — many trainers recommend front-clip for leash training. Downside: can interfere with natural movement for some dogs.

**Dual-clip harnesses** (like Ruffwear Front Range and Rabbitgoo) give you both options. Use back for relaxed walks, front for training or reactive moments.

### How to Measure for a Dog Harness

Grab a soft measuring tape and measure:
1. **Chest girth** — the widest part of the chest, just behind the front legs (most important measurement)
2. **Neck girth** — just below the collar position
3. **Back length** — from the base of the neck to where the tail begins

Always check the manufacturer's size chart. Different brands use very different sizing standards — never assume your dog is the same size across brands.

### Signs a Harness Fits Correctly

- You should be able to slide two fingers under every strap
- The chest strap should sit across the breastbone, not the armpits or shoulders
- Your dog should be able to move their front legs freely with no rubbing
- They shouldn't be able to back out of it (test before hitting the streets!)

### No-Pull Harness vs Prong Collar vs Head Halter

For persistent pullers, harnesses are generally safer and more humane than prong collars or choke chains. Head halters (like the Gentle Leader) are also effective but require careful introduction. A front-clip harness combined with positive reinforcement training is the most trainer-recommended approach.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will a harness stop my dog from pulling?**
A harness alone won't eliminate pulling — training is still necessary. However, a front-clip harness makes it much easier to manage pulling and provides better leverage for training.

**Q: Can dogs wear harnesses all day?**
Most harnesses shouldn't be worn 24/7 — they can cause fur matting, skin irritation, or muscle imbalances. Remove the harness when your dog is home and resting.

**Q: Are harnesses better than collars?**
For walking, yes — especially for dogs who pull. Harnesses distribute pressure across the chest and don't put strain on the throat and trachea like collars can. Collars are still useful for ID tags.

**Q: How long do dog harnesses last?**
Budget options: 1-2 years. Premium brands like Ruffwear: 4-6+ years with proper care. Wash regularly (many are machine washable) and inspect for fraying or cracked buckles.
