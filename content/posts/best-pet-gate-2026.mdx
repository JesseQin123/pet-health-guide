## Types of Pet Gates: What You Need to Know

Before choosing a gate, you need to know which mounting style is appropriate for your situation.

### Pressure-mounted gates
Held in place by pressure against the wall (no screws). Advantages: no holes in walls, easy to install and remove, moveable. Limitations: not safe at the top of stairs (could be pushed out), not appropriate for very large or very determined dogs.

### Hardware-mounted gates
Screwed into the wall or door frame for permanent installation. Advantages: extremely secure, appropriate for top-of-stairs use, can't be pushed out by strong dogs. Limitations: requires drilling, leaves holes in walls when removed.

### Freestanding gates
No mounting required. Best for temporarily blocking large areas (living rooms, kitchen entrances). Can be moved by a determined dog.

## Choosing the Right Gate: Decision Guide

### What height do you need?

- **Small dogs (under 15 lbs)**: 24"–28" is usually sufficient
- **Medium dogs (15–40 lbs)**: 30"–34"
- **Large breeds (40–80 lbs)**: 36"–41"
- **Athletic/jumping breeds** (Border Collie, Husky, Standard Poodle): 41"+

### Is this for top of stairs?

If yes, **hardware-mounted only**. Pressure-mounted gates can be pushed out at the top of stairs with dangerous consequences. The Summer Infant Multi-Use and Evenflo Position & Lock (hardware mount) are both appropriate.

### How wide is the opening?

Measure the opening first. Most gates expand from about 29" to 34" in their base version, with extensions available for wider openings. The Regalo Extra-Wide and Cumbor cover wider openings with included extensions.

### Do you have multiple pets with different needs?

If you need cats to pass through while blocking dogs, the Carlson Extra-Tall has a built-in small pet door at the base.

## Installation Tips

**Pressure-mounted gates:**
1. Clean the wall surfaces where the gate will press (dust, paint texture can affect grip)
2. Apply the pressure spindles at an equal depth on both sides
3. Test by pushing firmly with your hand before trusting your pet to it
4. Re-check monthly — pressure can loosen over time, especially in temperature-changing homes

**Hardware-mounted gates:**
1. Locate wall studs for the primary anchor points
2. The hinged side should anchor into a stud; the latch side can use drywall anchors
3. For stair bannisters, use the Y-adapter that comes with most gates and tighten firmly
4. Test the latch and hinges before first use

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the best pet gate for large dogs?**
The **Carlson Extra-Tall Walk-Through Gate** (41") handles most large breeds. For dogs over 70 lbs that push or jump, add hardware mounting (use the Evenflo or Summer Infant) for added security.

**Can cats jump over a 30" gate?**
Yes, easily. Cats regularly jump 4–5 feet. If you need a cat barrier, look for 40"+ gates, or use a top-of-door cat gate that only leaves a small gap at the bottom for cats to pass through.

**Can I use a baby gate as a pet gate?**
Yes. Most "baby gates" and "pet gates" are identical products with different marketing. Check the height and weight rating for your specific dog — a 30" baby gate won't stop a jumping Labrador.

**How do I stop my dog from pushing over a pressure-mounted gate?**
First, ensure both spindles are fully tightened. If the dog is still pushing it out, switch to a hardware-mounted option. Alternatively, place the gate in a location where there's a baseboard on both sides providing extra grip surface.

**Are wire/metal gates better than plastic?**
Metal frames are more durable and harder to chew through. For small dogs and cats, plastic frames are typically sufficient. For medium to large dogs with chewing tendencies, metal frames and mesh are more appropriate.
