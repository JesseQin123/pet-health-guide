## Cat Carrier Buying Guide for Air Travel

### Soft-Sided vs. Hard-Sided for Air Travel

Hard-sided carriers are more protective but almost never fit under airline seats. **For in-cabin travel, always choose soft-sided.** Hard carriers should only be used when checking your pet as cargo — which we strongly advise against for cats unless absolutely necessary (cargo temperatures are unregulated and the experience is highly stressful).

### What "Airline Approved" Actually Means

"Airline approved" is a marketing claim, not a certification. It means the manufacturer claims the carrier meets typical airline dimensions. **The only way to be certain is to:**

1. Check your specific airline's current pet carrier policy
2. Measure the carrier yourself
3. Call your airline 24–48 hours before flying to confirm

Airline staff at the gate have the final say, not the carrier's packaging.

### Weight Limits: Your Cat + The Carrier

Most airlines charge $125–$150 in-cabin pet fee and have a combined cat-plus-carrier weight limit of 20 lbs (Delta, American) or 25 lbs (some others). Weigh your cat and the carrier together before you book.

### Preparing Your Cat for Flight

- **Before the trip:** Leave the carrier out for 2–3 weeks before flying. Put treats, toys, and bedding inside so your cat associates it with positive experiences.
- **Day of travel:** Don't feed your cat for 4–6 hours before the flight to reduce nausea. Give water up to 2 hours before.
- **During the flight:** Keep the carrier in your lap if the seat in front is too far for your cat to see/smell you. Speak calmly. Don't open the carrier mid-flight unless it's an emergency.
- **Consider:** Ask your vet about anti-anxiety options (Gabapentin, Feliway spray on the bedding) for extremely stressed cats.

### TSA Security: What to Expect

You must remove your cat from the carrier when going through security. The carrier goes through the X-ray machine alone. Have a plan:
- Practice removing your cat from the carrier at home so it's familiar
- Bring a backup harness with clip-on leash for the security transition
- Request a private screening room if your cat is aggressive or you're worried about escape

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use the carrier as a seat bag instead of under-seat?**

No. Airlines require in-cabin pets to remain under the seat in front of you for the entire flight, except during security check. The carrier may not go in the overhead bin.

**Does my cat need a health certificate to fly?**

Most domestic US airlines require a health certificate issued by a licensed vet within 10 days of travel. International flights have stricter and varied requirements. Check your airline's pet policy and destination country rules.

**What if my cat meows the whole flight?**

Tell your seatmates in advance and apologize preemptively — most people are understanding. A pheromone spray (Feliway) on the bedding 30 minutes before travel can reduce vocalization. Don't open the carrier or take the cat out — this increases anxiety for both of you.

**Can two cats share one carrier?**

Only if the carrier is large enough for both to stand and turn. Most airlines allow only one pet carrier per passenger. Check if you can book adjacent seats to bring two cats (each in their own carrier).
