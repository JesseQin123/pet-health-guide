## What Causes Food Allergies in Dogs?

Dog food allergies are almost always a reaction to a protein source — not grains, despite popular belief. The most common allergens are:

- **Beef** (most common)
- **Dairy**
- **Chicken**
- **Wheat**
- **Eggs**
- **Soy**
- **Lamb**

Dogs develop allergies through repeated exposure, which is why rotation diets (switching protein sources regularly) are recommended as a preventive measure. Breeds with higher allergy rates include Bulldogs, Golden Retrievers, Labrador Retrievers, German Shepherds, Boxers, and West Highland White Terriers.

## What to Look for in Dog Food for Allergies

When choosing food for a dog with allergies or a sensitive stomach, prioritize:

1. **Short ingredient lists** — fewer ingredients = fewer potential triggers
2. **Novel proteins** — proteins your dog hasn't eaten before (venison, duck, rabbit, pork, lamb)
3. **No artificial additives** — preservatives, colors, and flavor enhancers can worsen reactions
4. **Single protein source** — makes it easier to isolate the cause if a reaction occurs
5. **No common fillers** — corn, wheat, and soy are low-nutrition and potential allergens
6. **Hydrolyzed or limited antigen options** — for severe cases, vet-prescribed hydrolyzed protein diets may be needed

Fresh dog food scores well on almost all of these criteria compared to commercial kibble.

## How to Do an Elimination Diet with Fresh Dog Food

If your vet suspects a food allergy, an elimination diet is the gold standard for diagnosing it. Here's how to do it:

1. **Pick a fresh food brand with a novel protein** — choose a protein your dog has never eaten (for most dogs, this means pork, lamb, or duck rather than chicken or beef)
2. **Feed ONLY that food for 8–12 weeks** — no treats, no table scraps, no flavored medications; even trace amounts of allergens can interfere with the trial
3. **Document symptoms** — track itching, ear infections, GI issues, and coat condition weekly
4. **Challenge phase** — if symptoms clear, reintroduce the suspected allergen for 2 weeks to confirm the allergy
5. **Work with your vet** — especially if your dog has severe symptoms; hydrolyzed protein prescription diets may be needed for severe cases

Fresh dog food makes elimination diets far easier than trying to manage them with commercial kibble, because you can actually read and trust the ingredient list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How quickly will I see results after switching to fresh dog food for allergies?**
Digestive improvements (better stool quality, reduced bloating) often appear within 1–2 weeks. Skin and coat improvements from food allergy elimination typically take 6–8 weeks because the immune system needs time to clear the allergen response.

**My dog has been on the same food for years — can they develop allergies to it?**
Yes. Food allergies in dogs develop through repeated exposure, not first contact. A dog can eat chicken every day for 5 years and then develop a chicken allergy. This is why protein rotation is recommended.

**Is grain-free food better for allergic dogs?**
Not necessarily. Grains are rarely the primary allergen — proteins are. Grain-free foods can still contain common protein allergens (beef, chicken) and may actually cause issues for some dogs. The FDA has also noted a potential association between grain-free diets and dilated cardiomyopathy in dogs. Focus on the protein source, not grain content.

**Should I talk to my vet before switching to fresh food?**
Always a good idea, especially if your dog has existing health conditions. Most vets are supportive of fresh, AAFCO-compliant diets, but the transition process and any supplementation should ideally be discussed with your veterinarian.
