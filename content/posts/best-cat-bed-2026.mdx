## How to Choose the Right Cat Bed

### Match the bed to your cat's sleep style

Watch your cat for a week before buying. Patterns to note:
- **Burrowers** (hide under blankets, curl into tight balls): Cave bed or donut bed with high sides
- **Stretchers** (sleep spread out, on their sides or backs): Flat orthopedic bed or large mat
- **Perchers** (sleep on high surfaces, watch the room): Window perch or elevated cat tree perch
- **Cold cats** (huddle near radiators, sleep in patches of sun): Heated bed or self-warming bed

### Consider age and health

- **Kittens**: Any soft bed works; calming donut beds help with transitions
- **Adult cats (1–7 years)**: Personal preference rules; follow sleep style above
- **Senior cats (8+ years)**: Orthopedic support and low entry height are most important; add heated bed if joints are affected

### Washability matters

Cats shed. Beds need regular washing to prevent odor, allergen buildup, and flea infestations. Check that your chosen bed has a machine-washable cover before buying.

## Getting Your Cat to Actually Use the Bed

The most common complaint about cat beds is that the cat ignores them. This usually comes down to placement and introduction:

1. **Place near their current favorite spot**, not in an empty corner. If your cat sleeps on the couch, put the bed on the couch first.

2. **Add familiar scents**: Place a worn t-shirt or blanket in the bed for the first few days.

3. **Use catnip or silver vine**: Sprinkle on the bed to attract initial investigation.

4. **Don't force it**: Putting a cat in a bed and holding them there creates a negative association. Let them discover it on their own terms.

5. **Be patient**: Most cats take 3–7 days to adopt a new sleeping spot.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do cats actually use cat beds?**
Yes — when placed correctly. Cats that ignore beds are usually responding to poor placement (wrong location), wrong style (open bed for a cat that likes to burrow), or unfamiliar scent. Match the bed type to your cat's sleep style and introduce it gradually.

**How often should I wash a cat bed?**
Every 1–2 weeks for most households. Homes with allergies or multiple cats should wash weekly. A fresh-smelling bed is more appealing to cats and healthier for everyone.

**Are heated cat beds safe?**
Yes — when properly designed. Look for products that are MET listed or UL certified, and that include a thermostat (K&H's beds only heat when the cat is present). Never leave a heated bed on a surface that could overheat, and replace any bed with a frayed cord.

**What's the best cat bed for a senior cat?**
Orthopedic support + low step-in height + warmth option. The **K&H Thermo-Kitty Heated Bed** and **Furhaven Orthopedic Cat Bed** are both excellent for senior cats. If budget allows, get both — a heated bed for cold months and an orthopedic bed as the main daytime option.

**How do I know what size bed to buy?**
Measure your cat when fully stretched out (nose to tail base). Add 4–6 inches. That's the minimum bed length. For donut/round beds, the interior diameter should accommodate your cat's curled body plus a few inches.
