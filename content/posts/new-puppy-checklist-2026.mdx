Getting a new puppy is one of the most exciting things in the world. It's also a crash course in how many things you need to buy before that dog comes home.

This checklist covers every essential, organized by category, so you can shop intelligently rather than panic-buying from the pet store. We've included budget picks and upgrades for every category, with notes on what you can skip for now.

## Quick Summary: Core Puppy Supplies

| Category | Must-Have | Skip for Now |
|---|---|---|
| Feeding | Food, bowls, puzzle feeder | Automatic feeder (not for puppies) |
| Sleeping | Crate, crate pad, playpen | Fancy dog bed (wait until chewing phase is over) |
| Training | Training treats, leash, collar, clicker | Retractable leash (not for puppies) |
| Health | Vet appointment, flea/tick prevention | Most supplements (not needed initially) |
| Enrichment | Kong, chew toys, puzzle toys | Expensive toys (puppies destroy everything) |
| Grooming | Brush, nail clippers, toothbrush | Full grooming kit (learn the basics first) |

## Health and Vet Supplies

### First vet appointment (within 48–72 hours)
Schedule this before you bring your puppy home. A vet appointment in the first 3 days confirms:
- Overall health
- Parasite check
- Vaccine schedule review
- Breed-specific health notes

## Grooming Supplies

### Brush (breed-appropriate)
Brush type depends entirely on your dog's coat:
- **Short coat** (Lab, Beagle): rubber curry brush
- **Medium coat** (Golden, Border Collie): slicker brush + comb
- **Long or double coat** (Husky, Pomeranian): deshedding rake + slicker brush
- **Wire coat** (Terrier): pin brush

**Recommendation:** [Hertzko Self-Cleaning Slicker Brush](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=hertzko+self+cleaning+slicker+brush) works for most breeds ($15–$20)

For large breeds, a grooming table with a non-slip mat saves your back and teaches the dog to stand still. Most puppy owners skip this initially.

Use a gentle puppy shampoo — puppies' skin is more sensitive than adult dogs. Human shampoo strips natural oils.

## What NOT to Buy Right Away

- **Expensive dog beds**: Puppies chew. Wait until 12–18 months.
- **Retractable leashes**: Teaches pulling, breaks easily, not appropriate for puppies.
- **Automatic feeders**: Not for puppies — meal timing and human interaction during feeding supports bonding and behavior.
- **Too many toys**: Puppies get overwhelmed. Start with 4–5 toys and rotate.
- **Breed-specific supplements**: Most puppies don't need supplements. Feed a quality puppy food and let your vet guide additions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the most important thing to buy before a puppy?**
The crate, enzyme cleaner, and appropriately-sized collar with ID tag. The crate sets up for housebreaking success; the enzyme cleaner is needed from day one; the ID tag protects your puppy if they get loose.

**How much does it cost to set up for a new puppy?**
Budget $200–$400 for the core essentials (food, crate, basic supplies). You can spend more on premium options but the basics work well. Ongoing costs (food, vet care, flea prevention) run $100–$300/month depending on breed and location.

**Do I need a playpen AND a crate?**
They serve different purposes. The crate is for sleep and unsupervised time when you can't watch closely. The playpen is for giving your puppy more room to move without full house access. Both are valuable in the first 3–6 months.

**What food should I feed my new puppy?**
Ask your breeder or rescue what food they've been on and continue it for at least 7–10 days. If you want to switch, transition gradually. Look for a food with a named protein source first in the ingredient list, and a WSAVA-approved brand (Purina, Royal Canin, Hill's, Eukanuba) is well-researched.

## Bottom Line

The essentials for a new puppy come down to three things: a safe space (crate + playpen), a consistent routine, and the right tools for training and accident cleanup. Everything else can be added as you learn your specific dog's needs.

Start simple, prioritize the veterinarian relationship, and resist buying every gadget in the pet store. The puppy you bring home will teach you what they actually need in the first week.
