Arthritis and joint disease affect approximately **25% of dogs** — and up to 80% of dogs over age 8. If your dog is slowing down, reluctant to climb stairs, or stiff after rest, joint supplements might be one of the most impactful changes you can make.

Here's an evidence-based breakdown of what works.

---

## The Short List: Most Effective Joint Supplements

1. **Glucosamine + Chondroitin** — foundational, most researched
2. **Omega-3 (Fish Oil)** — reduces inflammation, strong evidence
3. **CBD Oil** — promising pain relief, helps with mobility
4. **Turmeric/Curcumin** — anti-inflammatory, good adjunct
5. **Green-Lipped Mussel** — unique fatty acid profile, underrated

---

## 1. Glucosamine + Chondroitin

**Evidence level: Strong ✅**

Glucosamine is the most well-studied joint supplement for dogs. It's a building block of cartilage, and supplementing may slow cartilage degradation and reduce pain.

- **Dose**: 20mg/kg/day of glucosamine; 15mg/kg/day of chondroitin
- **Timeline**: 4–8 weeks to see results
- **Forms**: Chews, capsules, liquid

**Top product**: Cosequin DS is the gold standard, widely recommended by vets.

---

## 2. Omega-3 Fatty Acids (Fish Oil)

**Evidence level: Strong ✅**

Fish oil's EPA and DHA reduce inflammatory cytokines — the same compounds that drive joint pain. Multiple studies show measurable improvement in mobility.

- **Dose**: 20–55mg EPA+DHA per pound of body weight daily
- **Source**: Cold-water fish (salmon, sardine, anchovies) — avoid flaxseed (poor conversion in dogs)
- **Forms**: Liquid oil, softgels

**Caution**: High doses can cause GI upset. Introduce slowly.

---

## 3. CBD Oil

**Evidence level: Promising ✅**

A 2018 Cornell University study found that 2mg/kg of CBD twice daily significantly reduced pain scores and improved mobility in dogs with osteoarthritis. Anecdotal evidence is overwhelming.

CBD works via the endocannabinoid system to modulate pain perception and reduce inflammation.

- **Dose**: Start at 0.2mg/kg twice daily, increase as needed

## 4. Turmeric / Curcumin

**Evidence level: Moderate ⚠️**

Curcumin (the active compound in turmeric) has strong anti-inflammatory properties in lab studies. Real-world bioavailability in dogs is limited without a fat or black pepper additive.

- **Dose**: 15–20mg/kg daily of curcumin with piperine or fat
- **Best form**: Specialized canine formulations with absorption enhancers

---

## 5. Green-Lipped Mussel (GLM)

**Evidence level: Moderate ✅**

New Zealand green-lipped mussels contain ETA (eicosatetraenoic acid), a unique omega-3 not found in fish oil. Several studies show improvement in pain and mobility scores in dogs.

- **Forms**: Freeze-dried powder, oil
- **Note**: More research needed, but promising and low risk

---

## What Doesn't Work

- **Shark cartilage**: Limited evidence, sustainability concerns
- **Generic "joint" chews** without proven ingredients: often underdosed
- **Vitamin C alone**: Dogs produce their own; supplementing rarely helps

## When to See a Vet

Supplements are most effective for mild-to-moderate arthritis and as a preventive measure. If your dog:
- Can't bear weight on a limb
- Cries out in pain
- Has rapid symptom progression

See a vet immediately. X-rays and prescription NSAIDs may be necessary.
