# Best Cat Insurance 2026 — Lemonade vs Pumpkin vs Trupanion Compared

Cat insurance is one of the most overlooked financial products in pet ownership. Only about 4% of U.S. cat owners carry pet insurance — compared to roughly 20% of dog owners — despite cats routinely racking up vet bills that rival their canine counterparts.

The reason cat owners skip insurance is usually one of two assumptions: either "my cat is healthy" or "it's probably not worth it." Both assumptions tend to shatter around the $2,500–$4,000 vet bill that comes after a urinary blockage, hyperthyroidism diagnosis, or emergency surgery. That's not a rare scenario. Urinary tract disease is the #1 reason cats visit emergency vets, and hyperthyroidism affects an estimated 10% of cats over age 10.

## Why Cat Insurance Is Different from Dog Insurance

Before comparing plans, a few cat-specific notes:

- **Breed risk varies less than in dogs.** Domestic shorthairs, Maine Coons, Siamese, and Persians all have specific health tendencies, but the spread is narrower than across dog breeds. Insurers price accordingly.
- **Indoor cats still get sick.** "Indoor-only" doesn't protect against dental disease, urinary issues, hyperthyroidism, kidney disease, or cancer.
- **Age limits matter.** Some insurers cap enrollment at age 8 or 9. If you have a 7-year-old cat, compare carefully — this may be your last open enrollment window.
- **Pre-existing conditions are almost universally excluded.** Enroll young if you can.

## Cat Insurance Buying Guide: What to Look For

### 1. Annual Limit
Many cat emergencies (urinary blockage with hospitalization, cancer treatment) cost $3,000–$8,000. A $5,000 annual limit may sound sufficient but can fall short if your cat has multiple issues in the same policy year. Aim for $10K minimum; unlimited is ideal for cats prone to chronic conditions.

### 2. Deductible Type
- **Annual deductibles** (Lemonade, Pumpkin): You pay the deductible once per policy year, then the insurer covers its share of all remaining claims. Good for cats with multiple unrelated issues per year.
- **Per-condition deductibles** (Trupanion): You pay once per condition, ever. Good for chronic illness where one condition drives ongoing costs.

### 3. Reimbursement Percentage
70% is the floor; 90% is standard for good plans. The difference on a $5,000 vet bill: $1,500. Don't optimize on premium alone — the reimbursement rate matters.

### 4. Waiting Periods
Most plans have 14-day waiting periods for illness. Enroll before a health scare, not after. Pre-existing conditions (anything diagnosed before or during the waiting period) are excluded.

### 5. What's Excluded
Read the exclusions. Common exclusions across all providers:
- Pre-existing conditions
- Elective procedures (declawing, cosmetic)
- Breeding-related costs
- Some dental (varies by plan)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is cat insurance worth it for an indoor cat?**
A: Yes — especially for urinary tract issues, which disproportionately affect indoor cats fed dry food. A single urinary blockage hospitalization averages $2,000–$4,000.

**Q: What age should I get cat insurance?**
A: As early as possible. Most insurers enroll kittens from 8 weeks. Premiums are lowest when cats are young, and you avoid pre-existing condition exclusions for conditions that develop later.

**Q: Does cat insurance cover dental cleanings?**
A: Routine cleanings are typically not covered. Dental illness (infections, extractions) is covered by Lemonade and Pumpkin with the right plan tier.

**Q: Can I use any vet?**
A: Yes for all three providers above — they're not network-restricted. Trupanion also offers direct payment at participating vet clinics.

**Q: What's the difference between a $100 and $500 deductible?**
A: With an annual deductible, the lower deductible saves money if your cat has significant claims but costs more per month in premium. If your cat is young and healthy, a $500 deductible with lower premiums often makes sense. Run the math: (premium difference × 12) vs. deductible difference.
