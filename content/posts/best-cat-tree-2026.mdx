## How to Choose the Right Cat Tree

### Match the height to your cat's age and health

- **Kittens and young adult cats** (under 7): Go tall. 60–72" trees give them room to burn energy.
- **Senior cats or cats with joint issues**: Look for lower platform heights (under 18" between steps) and wider steps for easier climbing.
- **Large breeds** (Maine Coons, Ragdolls, Siberians): Prioritize weight capacity and wide perches (at least 16" × 16").

### Match stability to your cat's personality

Wobbly trees are rejected by most cats after one bad experience. If your cat is energetic or large, prioritize trees with wider bases, anti-topple kits, or solid wood construction. Budget trees often have narrow bases that tip easily.

### Sisal rope vs. sisal fabric

Sisal rope (twisted) is more durable and more satisfying to scratch than flat sisal fabric. Check which is used on the posts — this affects how long the tree remains attractive to your cat.

### Consider the number of cats

Most trees are rated for one or two cats. In multi-cat households:
- Choose trees with multiple separate perches (cats rarely share willingly)
- Look for enclosed condos that give a cat a "private" retreat
- Add a second tree if you have 3+ cats

## Cat Tree Setup Tips

**Placement matters:** Cats want to see what's happening. Place the tree near a window or in the main living area — not tucked in a corner or back bedroom. Window perches dramatically increase tree use.

**First introduction:** Don't move your cat to the tree. Sprinkle catnip on the perches, dangle toys from the levels, and let your cat discover it naturally over 24–48 hours.

**Stability check:** Before your cat uses it, give the top perch a firm shake with your hand. If it wobbles significantly, tighten all bolts and consider anchoring the top to the wall.

**Keep it clean:** Vacuum perches weekly and spot-clean with an enzyme cleaner as needed. Most perch covers are removable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How tall should a cat tree be?**
Taller is generally better — most cats want to be as high as possible. A 60–72" tree lets cats climb to true "top of the room" height. Shorter trees (under 48") are often underused by active cats.

**Do cats actually use cat trees?**
Yes — when placed in the right location (near a window, in an active room) and introduced gradually. Cats that ignore trees are often responding to poor placement or a tree that wobbles.

**How long do cat trees last?**
Budget trees: 1–3 years with regular use. Mid-range trees: 3–5 years. Premium solid wood trees (Molly and Friends, Armarkat heavy-duty): 5–10+ years.

**Can I refurbish a worn cat tree?**
Yes. Sisal rope replacement kits are available on Amazon for $15–25 and can be rewrapped around worn posts. Replacement platforms and covers are also available for most major brands.

**What's the best cat tree for large cats?**
Look for platforms at least 16" × 16", solid wood or thick fiberboard posts (not cardboard tubes), and weight ratings of 20+ lbs per platform. The Go Pet Club 72", Armarkat Classic, and Molly and Friends are best for big cats.
