## What to Look for in a Dog GPS Tracker

**Coverage network:** Most US trackers run on AT&T or T-Mobile. Check coverage maps for your specific area, especially if you live rurally.

**Subscription cost over time:** A $49 device with a $13/month subscription costs $205/year in total. Factor this in — the "cheap" tracker often isn't cheap long-term.

**Battery life:** Live tracking drains battery fast. A device rated 30 days with standard settings might only last 2–3 days in continuous live mode. Know your real use case.

**Size and weight:** GPS units add bulk. For dogs under 20 lbs, look for lightweight options (Tractive excels here). For large breeds, this matters less.

**Escape alerts:** All three trackers send immediate push notifications when your dog leaves a geofenced zone. Test the latency — Fi and Tractive edge out Whistle by a few seconds in our testing.
