## Why Regular Cleaners Fail (And Why Pets Return)

Pet urine contains urea, uric acid, creatinine, and proteins. When urine dries, the uric acid crystalizes into salt crystals. Those crystals bond to carpet fibers, grout, and wood grain. When humidity rises — from mopping, rain, or your pet's own body heat as they sniff the area — the crystals reactivate and re-emit odor.

Conventional cleaners:
- **Dish soap / vinegar**: Clean surface residue, dilute some odor, but don't break down uric acid crystals
- **Hydrogen peroxide**: Bleaches surface stain, doesn't eliminate odor compounds
- **Febreze / fabric sprays**: Mask odor with fragrance — the crystals remain

Enzyme cleaners work by deploying protease, amylase, lipase, and urease enzymes that biologically break down the protein, starch, fat, and uric acid components. The crystals are digested, not masked.

**One caveat:** Enzyme cleaners need time. Unlike spray-and-wipe products, enzymes require 10–15 minutes of dwell time (some formulas benefit from 30 minutes on set stains). This is why the application method matters as much as the product.

## Buying Guide: What to Look For in an Enzyme Pet Cleaner

### Surface Compatibility
- **Carpet**: Any enzyme cleaner on this list works, but check for CRI certification for high-end rugs
- **Hardwood (sealed)**: Use sparingly — excessive moisture damages wood. Choose a formula rated for hardwood or use Nature's Miracle Hard Floor formula
- **Hardwood (unsealed)**: Enzyme cleaners can penetrate the wood grain; better to prevent accidents in these areas
- **Upholstery**: Saturate, but ensure the fabric can handle moisture. Test in an inconspicuous spot first

### Concentration vs. Ready-to-Use
Concentrates cost less per ounce but require measuring and mixing. Ready-to-use products cost more but are convenient for single-location spot treatment. For high-volume use, concentrate is more economical.

### Pet Safety
All products above are pet-safe when dry. During application, remove pets from the area. Citrus-based products (Angry Orange) should be used with ventilation — cats in particular can be sensitive to strong citrus scents during wet application.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will enzyme cleaners work on old, set stains?**
A: Yes, but they require more effort. Rehydrate the area with warm water first (helps reactivate the crystals), then apply generously, cover with a damp cloth, and let sit 20–30 minutes. Multiple treatments may be needed for very old stains.

**Q: Can I use enzyme cleaners on all fabrics?**
A: Most yes. Always spot test on colored fabrics — some enzyme formulas can slightly lighten dyes. Never use on silk or leather.

**Q: Why does my pet keep going in the same spot even after cleaning?**
A: The stain wasn't fully eliminated. Either the cleaner didn't reach deep enough (important on carpet — the urine soaks through the fiber, padding, and can reach the subfloor) or you used a non-enzyme product. Re-treat with an enzyme cleaner using generous saturation.

**Q: Are enzyme cleaners safe to use around cats?**
A: When dry, yes. During application, relocate pets. Angry Orange's citrus formula requires ventilation and some cats dislike the smell.

**Q: How long do I need to let the cleaner sit?**
A: Minimum 10 minutes for fresh stains; 20–30 minutes for set stains. Longer contact time = better enzyme action.
