## What "Pre-Existing Condition" Actually Means

Before comparing insurers, it's worth understanding what you're up against. Pet insurance companies define pre-existing conditions as **any illness, injury, or symptom that existed before your coverage start date** — including during the waiting period.

There are two types:

- **Curable pre-existing conditions:** Things like ear infections, UTIs, or diarrhea that can fully resolve with treatment. Many insurers will cover these again after a symptom-free waiting period (usually 6–12 months).
- **Incurable/chronic pre-existing conditions:** Ongoing conditions like diabetes, epilepsy, or most orthopedic issues. These are almost universally excluded for life.

**Bilateral conditions** (conditions that affect both sides of the body, like hip dysplasia or cruciate ligament disease) are an especially tricky category. If your dog tore their left ACL and it was treated, most insurers won't cover the right ACL — because they assume the other side is "at risk."

## What to Do If Your Dog Already Has a Condition

If your dog has been diagnosed with a chronic condition like diabetes or epilepsy, here's the honest truth: no standard pet insurance plan will cover it. What you can do:

1. **Enroll for everything else.** Even with exclusions, insurance can cover future accidents, cancer, or unrelated illnesses — costs that can run $3,000–$15,000.

2. **Look for "time-limited" policies** in the UK market (not common in the US, but worth knowing exists).

3. **Get records reviewed before buying.** Some insurers offer pre-enrollment reviews so you know exactly what's excluded before you pay a premium.

4. **Focus on the curable conditions.** If your dog had an ear infection 18 months ago with no recurrence, that's probably coverable under Lemonade or Embrace — get a quote and ask specifically.
